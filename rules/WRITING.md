# Universal writing rules

AI-written copy often feels familiar before it feels wrong. The signal is rarely one word. It is a cluster: an empty setup, an abstract claim, three polished fragments, a contrast slogan, and a neat summary that adds nothing.

## Do not

1. **Open with throat-clearing.** Cut “In today's fast-paced world,” “It is important to note,” and other setup that delays the point.
2. **Use fake contrast as insight.** Avoid “It is not just X, it is Y,” “More than X,” and “Not this. That.” State the actual claim and evidence.
3. **Inflate ordinary actions.** “Leverage,” “unlock,” “empower,” “reimagine,” and “revolutionize” need a measurable meaning or deletion.
4. **Manufacture consensus.** Replace “experts say,” “many believe,” and “industry leaders agree” with a named source or no attribution.
5. **Force everything into three.** Use the number of points the subject has. Do not add a third synonym to make a slogan land.
6. **Cycle synonyms to avoid repetition.** Repeating the correct noun is clearer than rotating through “platform,” “solution,” “ecosystem,” and “offering.”
7. **Narrate your interpretation.** Remove “This highlights,” “This underscores,” and “This is a testament to.” Show the fact and let it carry weight.
8. **Stack dramatic fragments.** One fragment can have force. A page full of “Faster. Smarter. Together.” reads generated.
9. **End by recapping.** Stop when the useful work is done. Do not append “In conclusion” or a sweeping restatement.
10. **Use em dashes.** Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses when the sentence needs them.

## Do instead

- Lead with the answer, action, observation, or tension.
- Prefer concrete nouns, direct verbs, quantities, dates, and named examples.
- Preserve the speaker's vocabulary, humor, uncertainty, and sentence length.
- Make transitions follow the argument, not a template.
- Verify quotations, numbers, titles, links, and attribution.
- Read the draft aloud. Break the rhythm where every sentence has the same shape.
- Apply the portability test: if the sentence could sit unchanged on a bank, fitness app, and AI startup, it needs specifics.

## Review questions

- What is this sentence doing that the previous sentence did not?
- Which claim would a skeptical reader ask us to prove?
- Did the tool erase an opinion or rough edge that carried the author's voice?
- Is the final paragraph necessary?
