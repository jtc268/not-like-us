# Lovable writing guide

Lovable usually writes inside a product-generation task. Weak copy can become structural because components are sized around it.

## Common defaults to reject

- Generic hero claims such as “Transform your workflow” or “Everything you need.”
- Audience sprawl that claims the product works for teams, creators, founders, and enterprises at once.
- Three benefit statements created to fill a three-column section.
- Fake testimonials, invented company marks, or metrics that look real.
- Repeated “seamless,” “powerful,” “intuitive,” and “all-in-one” labels.
- Identical heading, sentence, and CTA patterns across every section.

## Better moves

- Supply approved copy or a content inventory before layout generation.
- Name the user, situation, action, and outcome in the first screen.
- Mark demo data as demo data. Use neutral placeholders where evidence is missing.
- Let section count follow the user's questions, not a landing-page template.
- Test copy at realistic extremes, including long names, zero results, failures, and permission limits.
- Rewrite in the product's existing voice after the flow works.

## Prompt addition

```text
Use only facts supplied in the brief. Do not invent customers, quotes, metrics, integrations, or awards.
Write for the named user and task. Remove generic benefit language and any section created only to complete a template.
```
