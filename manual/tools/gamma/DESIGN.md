# Gamma design guide

Gamma maps content into flexible, auto-layout cards. Its official documentation describes cards as the core unit and notes that AI selects card layouts. The format is useful, but it can make every argument feel like a sequence of polished panels.

## Common defaults to reject

- One idea per card even when ideas need comparison, continuity, or shared evidence.
- Repeated title, short paragraph, image, and accent-block layouts.
- Rounded cards floating on tinted backgrounds with little hierarchy between them.
- AI-selected stock or generated imagery used to fill every card.
- Shortened copy that removes qualifications needed to understand the claim.
- Decks that read as webpages rather than presentations with pacing and reveal.

## Better moves

- Build the argument and evidence map before assigning cards.
- Combine material that belongs in one comparison, table, timeline, or annotated figure.
- Vary density based on the audience's reading conditions.
- Use images only when they add evidence, explanation, or a deliberate tone.
- Export and inspect typography, cropping, page breaks, and accessibility.
- Rehearse the deck. A card sequence is not automatically a talk.

## Prompt addition

```text
Map the argument before choosing card layouts. Do not force one idea into each card.
Use comparisons, tables, timelines, or annotated evidence when the relationship requires them.
Do not add decorative images to fill space. Preserve qualifications that affect the claim.
```
